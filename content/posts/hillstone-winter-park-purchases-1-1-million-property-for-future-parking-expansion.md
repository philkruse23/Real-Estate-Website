> "Complete demolition for future parking lot." — Description from the demolition permit filed with the city of Winter Park.

A $1.1 million real estate investment could soon make visiting one of Winter Park's most popular waterfront restaurants more convenient.

**Hillstone Restaurant Group Inc. has purchased a neighboring property at 1298 Beachview Avenue for approximately $1.1 million**, signaling plans to expand parking near its established restaurant overlooking Lake Killarney.

The Phoenix-based restaurant company acquired the 15,000-square-foot building in late August, according to Orange County property records reported by the _Orlando Business Journal_. The property sits adjacent to Hillstone's restaurant at 215 S. Orlando Avenue.

Following the acquisition, a demolition permit was submitted to the city of Winter Park describing the project as a complete demolition for a future parking lot.

Although construction timelines and the number of proposed parking spaces have not been disclosed, the purchase highlights the growing importance of convenient parking in Winter Park's competitive restaurant and commercial real estate market.

## Hillstone Winter Park Plans Parking Expansion

Hillstone Restaurant in Winter Park has long been recognized for its waterfront setting, upscale dining experience, and distinctive architecture inspired by Frank Lloyd Wright.

Located along Lake Killarney, the restaurant has served Central Florida diners since 1996. Its menu, atmosphere, and customer service have helped establish it as one of the area's notable dining destinations.

In 2017, _Business Insider_ named Hillstone the best steakhouse in Florida, recognizing its food, service, and ambiance.

However, popularity can create challenges, particularly when customers have difficulty finding convenient parking.

The acquisition of the neighboring Beachview Avenue property could provide a practical solution.

### How Additional Parking Could Improve the Dining Experience

Expanding parking near the restaurant could offer several advantages, including:

- Easier access for guests during peak dining periods.
- Reduced time spent searching for available parking.
- Improved arrival experiences for customers with reservations.
- Potentially better traffic circulation around the restaurant.
- Greater operational flexibility during busy periods.

While the anticipated benefits are clear, the final impact will depend on the parking lot's design, capacity, and access points.

Hillstone Restaurant Group has not publicly released those details.

## Historic Winter Park Building Slated for Demolition

The proposed parking project involves a property with ties to Winter Park's tourism history.

According to Orange County records, the building at 1298 Beachview Avenue was constructed in 1941.

Before its conversion into office space, the property was associated with the **Lake Shore Motor Court**, a hotel operated by the Henderson family.

During the 1950s, the motor court attracted affluent tourists visiting the Winter Park area, according to historical reporting by _Winter Park Magazine_.

The property last changed hands in 1978 for approximately $41,000 before Hillstone's recent $1.1 million acquisition.

### Previous $50 Million Hotel Development Faced Opposition

The Beachview Avenue property has also been involved in an earlier redevelopment proposal.

In 2021, developers abandoned plans for the approximately $50 million Henderson Hotel, a proposed 132-room development involving the Beachview Avenue site and adjacent property at 250 Killarney Drive.

The project faced opposition from residents and city officials who raised concerns about:

- Increased traffic in surrounding neighborhoods.
- Additional noise from hotel operations.
- Expanded commercial development along Lake Killarney.
- Potential effects on the area's established residential character.

The Hillstone parking proposal represents a significantly different approach to the property.

Rather than constructing a large hospitality development, the restaurant group plans to remove the existing structure and potentially create additional surface parking.

Although the smaller scope could generate fewer concerns than the previous hotel proposal, questions about historic preservation, landscaping, drainage, and neighborhood compatibility may still arise.

## Winter Park Parking Demand Continues to Shape Commercial Development

Hillstone's property acquisition reflects a broader challenge for restaurants and businesses operating in Winter Park.

Convenient parking remains a valuable feature in the city's commercial real estate market, particularly for businesses that depend on steady customer traffic.

Properties with dedicated parking can attract additional interest from restaurant operators and other commercial tenants.

One example is the property at 610 W. Morse Boulevard, which generated significant market attention partly because it offered more than 50 parking spaces.

Mecatos Bakery & Cafe announced plans to move into that location in 2026.

The property is less than a mile from Hillstone, illustrating how parking availability can influence commercial real estate decisions within the same market.

### Winter Park Plans New Downtown Parking Garage

The city of Winter Park is also pursuing broader parking improvements.

Plans have been discussed for a public-private development that would create a new downtown parking garage near City Hall.

The proposed project would provide at least **265 parking spaces**, along with a commercial component, on an existing surface parking lot behind City Hall.

A 2024 parking study identified more than 2,709 parking spaces available on weekdays and 2,787 on weekends across downtown streets and parking garages.

However, the overall supply of parking spaces does not necessarily eliminate accessibility challenges.

Parking availability, distance from destinations, customer convenience, and peak demand can all influence how easily visitors navigate commercial districts.

For restaurant operators, those considerations can directly affect the customer experience.

## Why Hillstone's $1.1 Million Investment Matters

The proposed parking expansion demonstrates how established restaurant businesses can invest in improving existing operations rather than expanding through new locations.

For Hillstone, purchasing adjacent property may offer strategic advantages that extend beyond the land's immediate use.

### Improving Customer Convenience

Easy access is an important part of the overall dining experience.

Additional parking could reduce inconvenience for guests and make the restaurant more accessible during high-demand periods.

### Strengthening the Existing Restaurant Location

The acquisition gives Hillstone control over an adjacent parcel of land, potentially creating opportunities to improve the restaurant's surrounding infrastructure.

Unlike property located farther away, the neighboring site could allow for parking that directly serves restaurant customers.

### Balancing Business Growth and Community Priorities

Winter Park's development history demonstrates the importance of balancing commercial investment with neighborhood expectations.

The cancellation of the Henderson Hotel project showed how traffic, noise, preservation, and waterfront development can become major community concerns.

Hillstone's proposed parking lot may face a different level of scrutiny, but its final design will still play a role in determining how well the project fits the surrounding area.

## What's Next for Hillstone's Winter Park Parking Project?

Several details about Hillstone's proposed parking expansion remain unknown.

The restaurant group has not announced a demolition start date, a construction schedule, or the number of parking spaces expected at the site.

It also remains unclear what additional permits or development approvals may be required before the parking lot can be completed.

The submitted demolition permit establishes the intended future use of the property, but it does not provide a complete construction plan.

Future filings and city reviews may offer additional information about the project's layout, landscaping, traffic access, and timeline.

## Final Thoughts: Hillstone's Investment Reflects Winter Park's Growing Parking Needs

Hillstone Restaurant Group's $1.1 million property acquisition represents a potentially significant improvement for one of Winter Park's established dining destinations.

By purchasing a neighboring building and proposing its demolition for additional parking, the company is taking steps that could improve accessibility and customer convenience.

At the same time, the project raises questions about the future of a property with historical ties to Winter Park's hospitality industry.

As the city continues addressing parking demands and managing commercial development, Hillstone's plans illustrate the ongoing relationship between business investment, customer experience, and neighborhood preservation.

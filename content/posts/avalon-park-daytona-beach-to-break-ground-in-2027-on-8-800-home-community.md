## A New Live-Work-Play Community in Daytona Beach

Developed by the team behind Avalon Park Orlando, the Daytona Beach project aims to create a walkable community where residents can live, work, shop, and enjoy recreational activities.

The development will span approximately 2,760 acres, with the first phase covering 750 acres.

Initial construction plans include:

- New residential neighborhoods
- Approximately 100,000 square feet of retail space
- A grocery store and shopping areas
- An education village with school facilities
- Parks, recreational amenities, and a future downtown district

The community's full development is expected to take 20 to 30 years, eventually reaching a scale comparable to the city of Winter Park.

## Major Economic Benefits for Volusia County

Avalon Park Daytona Beach is projected to bring significant investment and economic activity to the region.

Developers estimate that construction could support approximately **27,000 jobs over the life of the project**, generating more than $1.1 billion in wages.

Once completed, the community is projected to support nearly 3,000 full-time jobs and generate approximately $70 million annually in property tax revenue.

New residents are also expected to contribute roughly $153 million in annual consumer spending, benefiting local businesses and the surrounding economy.

## Traffic and Utility Concerns Remain

Despite its economic potential, the development has raised concerns among nearby residents.

Increased traffic along State Road 40 and West Granada Boulevard remains a major issue.

To help address congestion, approximately $125 million has been allocated for road infrastructure improvements.

Questions surrounding water and wastewater services have also created challenges, including legal disputes involving Ormond Beach.

These infrastructure issues will be important as the project moves toward construction.

## Final Thoughts

Avalon Park Daytona Beach represents a major step in Central Florida's continued growth.

With thousands of planned homes, new businesses, schools, and employment opportunities, the development could transform the Daytona Beach area over the coming decades.

However, its long-term success will depend on balancing economic development with transportation, infrastructure, and community needs.

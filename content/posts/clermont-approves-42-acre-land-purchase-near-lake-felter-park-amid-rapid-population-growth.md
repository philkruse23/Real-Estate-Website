> “Business doesn't wait for government.” — Clermont City Manager Rick Van Wagner

As Clermont continues to experience significant population growth and expanding commercial development, city officials are taking steps to secure land for future recreational opportunities and environmental preservation.

The Clermont City Council approved the purchase of approximately 42 acres along Citrus Tower Boulevard near Lake Felter Park for $625,000 on September 8, 2026. The acquisition comes as available land becomes increasingly scarce throughout one of Central Florida's fastest-growing communities.

The property, which consists primarily of wetlands, represents an opportunity for the city to expand its public land holdings while protecting natural areas near an established recreational destination.

Although the city has not finalized plans for the site, the purchase highlights the growing importance of balancing economic development, environmental conservation, and public recreation as Clermont's population continues to increase.

## Clermont Moves to Secure 42 Acres Near Lake Felter Park

The property, owned by Randall and Michael Langley, is located along Citrus Tower Boulevard near Lake Felter Park.

City officials accelerated the acquisition process after learning that another prospective buyer was interested in purchasing the land.

During an August 25 City Council meeting, City Manager Rick Van Wagner emphasized the urgency of securing the property before it could be acquired by another party.

The city initially proposed paying $600,000 for the land, with officials targeting an October 1 closing date. The council subsequently approved the purchase at $625,000.

At the approved price, the property costs approximately $14,881 per acre, compared with its appraised value of $642,000.

The acquisition would give Clermont additional lake frontage and greater flexibility in planning for the area's recreational and environmental needs.

However, the property's environmental characteristics distinguish it from traditional development sites.

### Most of the Property Consists of Wetlands

Approximately 32 of the property's 42 acres are wetlands, leaving about 10 acres of dry land.

While wetlands can limit conventional development, they also provide important ecological benefits.

These include natural stormwater management, wildlife habitat, water filtration, and support for surrounding lake ecosystems.

The property's combination of wetlands and dry land could create opportunities for recreational improvements alongside environmental conservation.

Potential uses could include walking trails, nature observation areas, educational spaces, or additional passive recreation, depending on environmental assessments and future city planning.

No specific improvements have been approved.

## Lake Felter Park Could Benefit From Additional Public Land

Lake Felter Park currently serves Clermont residents through a variety of recreational amenities, including basketball courts, tennis courts, athletic fields, and a playground.

The acquisition of nearby property could allow the city to consider expanding those opportunities as demand for public recreation increases.

Council Member Alison Strange described the proposed purchase as an exciting opportunity because of the property's location near the lake.

City officials also emphasized the importance of allowing residents to provide public comments about the acquisition.

### Potential Future Uses for the Property

Although the city has not identified a final use for the land, several possibilities could be evaluated as part of future planning.

- **Expanded recreational opportunities:** Additional amenities that complement existing facilities at Lake Felter Park.
- **Nature trails and walking paths:** Low-impact recreational access where environmental conditions permit.
- **Wetland conservation:** Protection of natural habitats and water resources.
- **Environmental education:** Opportunities for residents and students to learn about local ecosystems.
- **Long-term public land preservation:** Reserving property for future community needs as Clermont continues to develop.

Any improvements would require consideration of environmental regulations, construction costs, long-term maintenance, and community priorities.

## Clermont's Rapid Population Growth Increases Demand for Land

Clermont's expanding population is a major factor influencing land availability and future recreational needs.

According to U.S. Census Bureau figures cited in the original reporting, the city's population increased from 28,742 residents in 2010 to 52,812 in 2025.

That represents an increase of approximately 24,070 residents, or nearly 84%, over 15 years.

Such growth has helped attract new businesses, residential communities, healthcare facilities, and commercial investment.

However, rapid development also increases demand for parks, roads, utilities, schools, and other public infrastructure.

As available land becomes more limited, local governments face increasing pressure to plan for future community needs before suitable properties are developed for other purposes.

### Limited Land Availability Creates Urgency

Brad Parker, a local land broker with Southern Realty Enterprises, noted that the area surrounding the proposed acquisition has experienced substantial growth over the past decade.

Parker emphasized that the scarcity of large vacant properties could make future public land purchases more difficult.

> “If they are going to do something, the time is now.”

The observation reflects a broader challenge facing growing Central Florida communities.

As residential and commercial development expands, large undeveloped properties can become harder to find, particularly near established neighborhoods and recreational areas.

Land acquisition decisions must therefore account for current market conditions as well as anticipated public needs.

## Commercial Real Estate Development Highlights Clermont's Growing Land Values

Recent real estate transactions along the U.S. Highway 27 corridor demonstrate the area's continued commercial investment.

A 2.8-acre vacant commercial parcel at 3350 S. U.S. Highway 27 sold for $3.8 million to Atlanta-based Fourth Time Partners.

The property is planned for a 28,306-square-foot micro-hospital associated with Nutex Health.

Another significant transaction involved the Sprouts Farmers Market-anchored Hammock Ridge Commons shopping center at 3420 S. U.S. Highway 27.

The shopping center sold for $18.5 million in July 2026 to Konforte Management.

These investments illustrate the commercial activity accompanying Clermont's growing residential population.

## Recreation Impact Fees Would Fund the Acquisition

Clermont intends to fund the land purchase through its Recreation Impact Fees Fund.

The fund is supported primarily by fees paid by developers constructing new residential projects.

Recreation impact fees are intended to help finance eligible capital improvements associated with increased demand created by new development.

These improvements can include recreational land acquisition and certain park facilities.

Using this funding source would connect the acquisition to the city's broader efforts to accommodate population growth.

However, purchasing the property represents only one component of potential future investment.

If the city eventually develops additional recreational amenities, separate planning, environmental reviews, capital improvements, and maintenance funding could be necessary.

## Balancing Development With Environmental Preservation

The proposed acquisition illustrates a recurring challenge facing fast-growing communities throughout Florida.

Residential and commercial development can create employment opportunities, expand local services, and generate economic activity.

At the same time, continued growth increases demand for accessible public spaces and places greater pressure on natural resources.

Land containing wetlands presents a particularly important planning consideration because environmental preservation and public recreation may need to coexist.

For Clermont, the property near Lake Felter Park offers an opportunity to evaluate how those priorities can be incorporated into future land-use decisions.

The ultimate value of the acquisition will depend partly on how the property is managed, what public benefits it provides, and the costs associated with maintaining it.

### Important Questions for Clermont's Future Planning

Several considerations remain relevant as the city determines the property's future.

- How much of the land can accommodate public access without disrupting sensitive wetland ecosystems?
- Could the property support additional recreational amenities for nearby residents?
- What environmental restrictions or permitting requirements would influence future improvements?
- How much would long-term maintenance and environmental management cost?
- How will residents participate in decisions regarding the property's future?

Addressing these questions will help clarify the potential recreational and environmental benefits of the acquisition.

## What's Next for the 42-Acre Property Near Lake Felter Park?

The Clermont City Council approved the purchase on September 8, 2026, although the completion of the property's closing has not been independently confirmed.

The city's original goal was to complete the transaction by October 1.

Once ownership is finalized, attention can shift toward future land-use considerations and potential recreational planning.

City officials have not announced a construction schedule, final site plan, or specific recreational development project.

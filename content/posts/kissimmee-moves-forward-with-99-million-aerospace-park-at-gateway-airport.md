**Kissimmee is taking a major step toward expanding Central Florida's aviation industry with plans for a $99 million aerospace park at Kissimmee Gateway Airport.** The development would transform a former golf course into a modern aviation hub featuring aircraft hangars, industrial facilities, and infrastructure for electric air taxis.

The project aims to attract aerospace businesses, create jobs, and strengthen the region's growing aviation economy.

## Former Golf Course to Become Aerospace Hub

The former Kissimmee Municipal Golf Course, which closed in 2018, is moving closer to redevelopment after city commissioners approved a term sheet on October 6, 2026.

Located at 3103 Florida Coach Drive, the 110-acre property will be developed in phases by Kissimmee Airport Development Co.

### Planned Aerospace Park Facilities

The proposed development includes:

- Eight aircraft hangars measuring approximately 30,000 square feet each
- A terminal and charging facility for electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft, also known as air taxis
- A 105,000-square-foot warehouse
- Two industrial or office buildings measuring 106,800 and 123,700 square feet
- A convenience store

These facilities could position Kissimmee Gateway Airport as an increasingly attractive location for aviation, aerospace manufacturing, and emerging transportation technologies.

## Expanding Kissimmee Gateway Airport

The aerospace park is part of a broader effort to modernize and expand Kissimmee Gateway Airport.

Other major projects include:

- **Air traffic control tower:** A planned $18 million facility supported by a $10 million federal grant.
- **International aviation:** A proposed customs facility, with construction targeted to begin in 2027.
- **New hotel:** A $25 million, 122-room Hyatt Studios hotel expected to be completed by the end of 2026.

Together, these investments aim to improve airport operations, attract businesses, and expand aviation services throughout Central Florida.

## Economic Growth and Job Opportunities

The aerospace park could create new opportunities for aviation companies, manufacturers, engineers, and skilled workers.

Its planned facilities may also encourage private investment and strengthen partnerships within Florida's growing aerospace industry.

However, the project remains in development. Final lease approval, permitting, and Federal Aviation Administration authorization are still required before the redevelopment can fully proceed.

## Final Thoughts

Kissimmee's proposed $99 million aerospace park represents an important opportunity for Central Florida's aviation industry.

By transforming an unused golf course into an aviation-focused business center, the city hopes to attract new companies, support employment, and encourage long-term economic growth.

The project also reflects Central Florida's increasing investment in aerospace technology and advanced transportation.

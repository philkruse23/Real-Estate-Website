> "I think it's a moment where businesses need to consolidate to survive." — Thomas Ward, founder of Pig Floyd's and Pigzza

After 13 years in Orlando, **Pig Floyd's has closed its original location and merged with Pigzza** as owner Thomas Ward works to navigate rising restaurant costs and financial challenges.

The combined restaurant opened on September 18, 2026, at 1050 N. Mills Ave., across the street from the former Pig Floyd's location. The move follows Chapter 11 bankruptcy filings for both restaurant companies earlier this year.

## Key Takeaways

- Pig Floyd's closed its original Orlando location on September 6, 2026, after 13 years.
- Pig Floyd's and Pigzza now operate together at 1050 N. Mills Ave.
- Both restaurant companies filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in March 2026.
- Rising rent, insurance, food, and labor costs contributed to the consolidation.
- Ward confirmed that no jobs were lost because of the move.

## Why Did Pig Floyd's Close?

Rising operating expenses played a major role in Ward's decision to consolidate the two restaurants.

Insurance alone reportedly cost $4,000 per month at the former Pig Floyd's location. Combined with increasing rent, taxes, food costs, and labor expenses, maintaining separate locations became financially challenging.

Court filings show that Pig Floyd's Smokehouse LLC and Pigzza LLC reported estimated assets of no more than $50,000 and liabilities ranging from $1 million to $10 million.

The Chapter 11 filings allow the businesses to pursue financial restructuring while continuing operations.

## What's New at Pig Floyd's and Pigzza?

The combined restaurant brings together popular dishes from both concepts, including Pig Floyd's Mexican street corn fries and Pigzza's cheesy bread.

New offerings include a smoked cheeseburger and birria pasta. The restaurant also features a full-service bar, with both sit-down and fast-casual dining options.

Ward plans to improve the outdoor space with picnic tables and covered seating to accommodate more community events.

The restaurant's announced hours are 11 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily, with a late-night walk-up window on Fridays and Saturdays from 11 p.m. to 2 a.m.

## A Bigger Challenge for Orlando Restaurants

Pig Floyd's consolidation reflects the financial pressures facing independent restaurants in 2026.

According to the National Restaurant Association, restaurant operators continue to face uneven customer traffic and increasing expenses. Meanwhile, growing demand for delivery has changed how customers experience dining.

For Ward, the merger is about more than reducing expenses. It is also an opportunity to restore the personal connections that make restaurants meaningful.

> "I want to make sure that where it's headed is a place where I want to continue being at," Ward said.

## What's Next for Pig Floyd's?

Although its original location has closed, Pig Floyd's continues serving customers alongside Pigzza.

The merger represents Ward's effort to lower operating costs, preserve both brands, and create a stronger connection with Orlando's dining community.

As independent restaurants face growing financial challenges, the Pig Floyd's and Pigzza consolidation highlights how adapting business operations may be essential for survival.

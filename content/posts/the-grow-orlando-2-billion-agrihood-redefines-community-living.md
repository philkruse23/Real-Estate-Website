## What Is an Agrihood?

An **agrihood** is a residential community built around a working farm, community garden, or agricultural space. Unlike traditional subdivisions that typically emphasize clubhouses, swimming pools, and recreational facilities, agrihoods make farming and nature central to the neighborhood experience.

The concept encourages residents to connect with locally grown food, participate in agricultural activities, and spend more time outdoors.

Communities such as Serenbe near Atlanta and Arden in Palm Beach County have helped popularize this approach, demonstrating how agriculture can become part of modern residential living.

The Grow brings this concept to Central Florida on a particularly ambitious scale, combining thousands of homes with agricultural facilities and preserved natural landscapes.

## Inside The Grow Orlando: A Community Built Around Nature

Located at 16735 Barnview Drive, near the University of Central Florida, The Grow covers approximately 1,200 acres in East Orange County.

The master-planned development is expected to include 2,200 residential units alongside retail spaces, recreational amenities, and a working agricultural campus.

### A Farm-to-Table Lifestyle

At the center of The Grow is its agricultural concept, featuring a working farm, greenhouses, community gardens, and a harvest barn.

These facilities are intended to introduce residents to local food production while creating opportunities for agricultural education and community participation.

Rather than treating farming as a decorative feature, the development aims to incorporate it into everyday neighborhood life.

This approach could encourage residents to develop stronger connections with their food sources while promoting greater awareness of sustainable agricultural practices.

### Green Spaces and Outdoor Amenities

Environmental preservation is another defining element of The Grow.

The development plans to preserve approximately 60% of its land as green space, complemented by 28 miles of biking and jogging trails.

Planned recreational amenities include a resort-style swimming pool, fitness center, playgrounds, parks, and a dog park.

These features are designed to encourage outdoor activities while providing shared spaces where neighbors can interact.

The combination of agricultural landscapes and recreational facilities distinguishes The Grow from conventional residential developments.

## The Grow Orlando Homes: Modern Design Meets Farmhouse Charm

Pulte Homes is leading residential construction at The Grow, offering properties that combine vintage farmhouse-inspired architecture with contemporary floor plans.

The community's initial offerings included 25 home designs, ranging from approximately 1,628 square feet to more than 4,270 square feet.

Homes were originally priced between $400,000 and $840,000, with layouts featuring three to six bedrooms.

Featured models include the Merlot Grand, a spacious five-bedroom single-family home; the Coral Grand, a four-bedroom residence; and the Highgate, a more compact single-family design.

Additional options include the Caden bungalow and Trailwood townhome.

This variety provides options for buyers with different household sizes, space requirements, and lifestyle preferences.

The development generated early interest, with Pulte Homes reporting 85 home sales before the community's September 2026 grand opening.

However, buyers should confirm current pricing, availability, and construction schedules directly with the builder.

## Why The Grow Matters for Orlando's Real Estate Market

As Orlando continues to grow, housing developments face increasing pressure to balance residential demand with environmental preservation and quality of life.

The Grow introduces a model that integrates housing, agriculture, and recreation into one community.

### A Different Approach to Suburban Development

Traditional suburban communities frequently prioritize residential construction and standard recreational amenities.

The Grow places agricultural spaces and natural landscapes at the center of its master plan.

This approach reflects growing interest in neighborhoods that offer experiences beyond conventional housing.

Its proximity to the University of Central Florida may also appeal to faculty members, employees, and families seeking housing in East Orlando.

### Sustainability and Community Connection

The development's emphasis on preserved green space and agricultural activities could offer environmental and social benefits.

Access to parks and trails encourages outdoor recreation, while shared gardens and community spaces create opportunities for residents to connect.

However, long-term sustainability depends on more than attractive landscaping.

Water conservation, responsible land management, transportation infrastructure, and the continued operation of agricultural facilities will be important factors in determining the project's environmental impact.

## What Homebuyers Should Consider

Although The Grow offers a distinctive residential lifestyle, prospective buyers should evaluate several practical factors before purchasing.

Because the development will be completed in phases, not all planned amenities may be available immediately.

Buyers should also review homeowner association fees, insurance costs, property taxes, and any additional community expenses.

Understanding how the working farm operates is equally important, particularly regarding access to produce, agricultural programming, and potential participation fees.

These considerations can help buyers determine whether the community aligns with their expectations and financial goals.

## Could Agrihoods Shape the Future of Residential Living?

The Grow represents a growing movement toward communities that combine residential convenience with agricultural experiences and access to nature.

Its long-term success will depend on whether the development delivers its planned amenities while maintaining the environmental and community-focused values behind the agrihood concept.

If successful, The Grow could provide a useful model for future master-planned developments throughout Central Florida and beyond.

## Final Thoughts: Growing More Than Homes

The Grow Orlando is more than a $2 billion residential project. It represents an ambitious effort to rethink how neighborhoods integrate nature, agriculture, and everyday living.

With thousands of planned homes, extensive green spaces, and a working farm, the development offers an alternative vision for suburban communities.

As construction progresses, The Grow may provide important insights into the future of sustainable residential development.

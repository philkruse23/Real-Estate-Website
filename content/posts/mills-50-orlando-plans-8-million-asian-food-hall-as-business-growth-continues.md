## Former Post Office to Become Asian Food Destination

The proposed Mills Avenue Food Hall will transform a former 6,234-square-foot postal facility into an approximately 11,822-square-foot, two-story development.

Developer Patrick Dang envisions an affordable and welcoming destination where residents and visitors can explore a variety of Asian culinary experiences.

The ground floor will feature food vendors, while the second floor will provide flexible professional office suites.

The project remains in the early permitting stage, with no confirmed opening date.

## New Restaurants Continue to Arrive in Mills 50

Mills 50 is already recognized for its diverse restaurants, colorful murals, independent businesses, and walkable atmosphere.

Several new concepts have been announced for the district, including:

- **Little Sister Dumpling:** A new dumpling-focused restaurant at 713 N. Mills Avenue.
- **Krispy Rice:** A sushi concept specializing in crispy rice creations.
- **Uji Matcha Cafe:** A cafe offering traditional and modern matcha drinks.
- **Eastwood Orlando:** A restaurant featuring live-fire cooking and wood-grilled dishes.
- **Robin's Creamery:** A dessert shop featuring mochi, matcha, and boba.
- **Rews on Mills:** A new cocktail bar and lounge.

These planned openings highlight the area's growing reputation as a destination for food lovers.

## Mills 50's Strong Commercial Real Estate Market

Retail demand remains strong in the Mills 50 area.

According to Cushman & Wakefield's second-quarter 2026 report, the retail submarket that includes Mills 50 recorded a vacancy rate of just **2.3%**, compared with Orlando's metro-wide average of 3.9%.

Average asking rents were approximately $27.03 per square foot, below the metro average of $31.18.

The combination of relatively low vacancy, walkability, and diverse dining options continues to attract entrepreneurs and investors.

## What the Development Means for Orlando

The proposed food hall could create opportunities for local chefs, restaurant operators, and small businesses.

It may also attract additional visitors, support employment, and strengthen Mills 50's identity as a cultural and culinary destination.

However, the project's success will depend on permitting, construction, and attracting food vendors.

## Final Thoughts

The proposed $8 million Asian food hall represents another step in Mills 50's continued transformation.

With new restaurants, strong retail demand, and growing investment, the neighborhood remains an important part of Orlando's evolving dining and business scene.

Orlando is moving forward with a **$35.7 million parking garage near Inter&Co Stadium**, a major infrastructure investment that could create more than 800 parking spaces and open the door for future mixed-use development in downtown Orlando.

The project, planned for the city-owned Grove Park property, represents more than an expansion of downtown parking capacity. It could also support new residential units, retail businesses, and commercial activity on surrounding land.

## Orlando Moves Forward With $35.7 Million Grove Park Parking Garage

The Orlando City Council approved ranking Finfrock as the top firm to negotiate a design-build contract for the proposed Grove Park parking garage.

The project would replace the existing surface parking lot, which currently contains 627 parking spaces.

The proposed structure would include:

- At least 800 parking spaces, potentially within a seven-story structure.
- Approximately 22,000 square feet of ground-floor office space for downtown clean team and ambassador operations.
- Parking access for visitors attending events at Inter&Co Stadium and the Kia Center.
- Reserved parking for Travel + Leisure and courthouse employees.

At its minimum proposed capacity, the new garage would increase parking availability by approximately 173 spaces compared with the existing surface lot.

Beyond increasing parking capacity, the project would consolidate vehicle parking into a multistory structure, potentially allowing other parts of the property to accommodate higher-density development.

### Why Finfrock Was Selected

Finfrock ranked ahead of Wellbro Building Corp. and McCree Design Builders Inc. during the city's selection process.

The selection committee identified Finfrock's understanding of the project's scope as a key factor in its decision.

However, the ranking does not represent final approval of a construction contract.

Finfrock must negotiate an agreement with city staff, while Orlando City Council will separately review and approve the design and construction phase agreements.

If negotiations are unsuccessful, the city may proceed with the next-ranked firm.

## Future Mixed-Use Development Could Transform the Grove Park Property

One of the most significant opportunities associated with the parking garage involves the surrounding city-owned land.

Orlando has assembled approximately 6.75 acres through multiple property acquisitions.

These purchases include:

- **2021:** The city acquired 5.5 acres along Grove Park Drive for $9.5 million.
- **2025:** Orlando purchased an additional half-acre property for $1.4 million.
- **1993:** The city acquired a 0.86-acre vacant parcel at 548 W. Washington St. for $78,000.

According to city documents, the northern portion of the property has been identified as a potential mixed-use development site.

### Proposed Concept Includes 440 Apartments and Retail Space

One preliminary development concept included in the city's proposal outlines:

- 440 market-rate and affordable apartment units.
- 44,800 square feet of ground-floor retail space.
- A combination of residential and commercial uses near downtown entertainment venues.

Although the concept has not been approved as a formal development project, it illustrates how the surrounding property could be transformed into a residential and commercial destination.

The parking garage could support this future development by providing centralized parking infrastructure and improving the site's attractiveness to potential private investors.

However, the feasibility of future projects would depend on market conditions, development costs, financing availability, and city approvals.

## Downtown Orlando Population Growth Could Drive Development Demand

Orlando's growing downtown population is another factor supporting the city's long-term development plans.

City projections estimate that downtown Orlando's population will increase from 19,501 residents in the 2020 Census to approximately 25,008 by 2030.

That represents an estimated **28% population increase** over the decade.

As downtown's residential population expands, demand could rise for apartments, neighborhood retail, restaurants, services, and transportation infrastructure.

The potential development of 440 additional apartments near Grove Park could help accommodate some of that growth while increasing activity around nearby entertainment destinations.

### How Parking Infrastructure Can Support Urban Growth

Structured parking facilities can contribute to redevelopment strategies by reducing dependence on large surface parking lots.

Rather than dedicating extensive property to ground-level parking, multistory garages can consolidate vehicle storage and potentially free nearby parcels for more productive uses.

For downtown Orlando, that could create opportunities for:

- Higher-density residential development.
- New retail and restaurant businesses.
- Improved access to entertainment destinations.
- Increased pedestrian activity and commercial investment.
- More efficient use of publicly owned land.

The project's long-term impact would also depend on how effectively future development integrates pedestrian connections, public spaces, and transportation options.

## Could the Parking Garage Attract More Private Investment?

The Grove Park parking garage could help establish infrastructure needed to attract future investment, although additional development is not guaranteed.

Damien Madsen, founder of MadsenSmith Property Services, suggested that the garage might not immediately generate new development but could make the northern portion of the property more attractive over time.

That outlook reflects how public infrastructure projects can influence private real estate investment.

By providing parking access and supporting nearby destinations, the garage could reduce certain logistical challenges for future development proposals.

However, developers would still need to evaluate market demand, construction costs, financing, and potential returns before moving forward.

### Economic Development Opportunities and Challenges

The proposed garage presents several potential economic development benefits, including improved event parking, support for nearby businesses, and the possibility of future housing and commercial construction.

At the same time, the city will need to evaluate several important considerations.

**Financial performance:** The $35.7 million investment will require an assessment of long-term operating expenses, parking revenue, maintenance costs, and public benefits.

**Future land use:** Development plans for the remaining property will help determine whether the site can accommodate residential growth, affordable housing, and neighborhood-serving businesses.

**Downtown accessibility:** Pedestrian infrastructure, transit connectivity, and street-level activity will influence how successfully the garage integrates with surrounding development.

These factors could shape the project's long-term economic value beyond its immediate parking function.

## Orlando Continues to Modernize Downtown Parking

The Grove Park project is part of Orlando's broader effort to manage parking demand as downtown continues to develop.

The city oversees approximately 9,000 off-street parking spaces in garages and surface lots, along with another 1,200 paid on-street spaces.

Orlando has also introduced digital parking initiatives and increased parking rates in recent years to encourage more efficient use of existing infrastructure.

The proposed garage would expand parking access for major downtown destinations while supporting the city's phased development strategy.

As downtown Orlando continues to grow, balancing parking availability with housing development, pedestrian accessibility, and efficient land use will remain an important consideration for city planners.

## What's Next for the Grove Park Parking Garage?

Finfrock will negotiate the design-build contract with city staff before the project moves into its next stage.

The current anticipated schedule includes:

- **Early 2027:** Beginning of the design phase.
- **Future council meetings:** Approval of design and construction phase agreements.
- **End of 2028:** Targeted completion of construction.

Additional mixed-use development on the surrounding property would likely proceed through separate planning, financing, and approval processes.

Although no formal timeline has been announced for the potential apartments and retail space, the parking garage could serve as an important first phase in the property's redevelopment.

## Final Thoughts: A New Opportunity for Downtown Orlando Development

Orlando's proposed $35.7 million Grove Park parking garage represents a significant infrastructure investment with potential implications for the city's real estate market.

While the project will provide more than 800 parking spaces and improve access to major downtown venues, its broader significance could lie in the development opportunities surrounding the structure.

The possibility of adding 440 apartments and nearly 45,000 square feet of retail space highlights the property's potential to support additional housing, commercial activity, and private investment.

Whether those plans ultimately materialize will depend on market demand, development feasibility, and future city decisions.

For now, the Grove Park project represents another step in Orlando's efforts to prepare downtown for continued population growth and economic development.
